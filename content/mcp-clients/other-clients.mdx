## Sign in

A client that speaks OAuth discovers Stunt Double's sign-in endpoints from the server, registers itself, and opens your browser. With `mcp-remote`, the bridge does this for the client the first time it starts. Either way, sign in, review the scopes the consent screen lists, and approve. The [overview](/mcp#authentication) explains each scope.

## Check it works

- Ask: "List my Stunt Double workspaces." An answer with your workspaces means you're signed in.
- If your client shows MCP resources, open `stuntdouble://connection` to see the scopes the connection holds.

## Troubleshooting

- **mcp-remote needs Node 18 or later.** Desktop apps use the system Node, not one from a version manager in your shell.
- **Start the sign-in over:** `mcp-remote` stores its tokens in `~/.mcp-auth`. Delete that folder and restart the client. Do the same after a "Token exchange failed" error.
- **Port conflict during sign-in:** `mcp-remote` listens for the OAuth redirect on a local port. Pass a different port as the argument after the server URL.
- **Too many tools for the client:** connect to the on-demand endpoint in [Connect](/mcp/connect#endpoints) instead.
