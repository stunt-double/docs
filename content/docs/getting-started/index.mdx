# Quickstart

This guide takes you from a new account to your first checklist run, then
shows you how to make your own actor and checklist.

<PageFacts
  items={[
    { label: "Time", value: "About 5 minutes" },
    { label: "Who", value: "Anyone in a workspace" },
    { label: "You need", value: "A URL an actor can reach" },
  ]}
/>

<Steps>
<Step>

### Create a workspace

When you first sign in, Stunt Double asks you to create a **workspace**. It
holds your projects, members and billing, so name it after your team or
company. If a colleague's workspace accepts your email domain, you can join it
instead.

Sign-in is passwordless: enter the six-digit code sent to your email, or
continue with Google.

</Step>
<Step>

### Tell it what to test

Next, enter your product's URL. Stunt Double reads the site, writes actors for
the people who use it and picks the flows worth checking first, as checklists
in your first **project**. It takes a couple of minutes, and the project also
gets a first agent readiness score from the
**[Stunt Double Index](/index-ranking)**.

No URL yet? Choose **I don't have a URL yet** and describe the product and who
it is for instead. Checklists run as soon as you add a URL to the project.

When setup finishes, choose **Watch your first run** to see an actor work
through the first checklist live.

A project keeps related actors, checklists and runs together, so a team with
several products keeps them apart. Its overview shows the latest run step by
step, anything running now and what changed since your last visit.

<Screenshot
  name="dashboard"
  alt="A project in the Stunt Double dashboard: the sidebar lists interviews, comments, checklists and automations, beside the project's checklists and their latest results."
  caption="A project's home. Everything you create from here on lives inside it."
/>

</Step>
<Step>

### Create an actor of your own

Setup gives you a starting cast, but you can add actors at any time. An
**actor** is an AI user that does the work. Open **Actors** in the sidebar,
choose **Create actor**, then pick a template or **Write your own**. Give it:

- A **name** that describes a role, such as "Busy shop owner".
- **Instructions** that describe who it is and how it behaves.

After it is created, its **Knowledge** tab holds what it can look up: facts
about your product, test accounts, processes to follow.

Out of the box an actor drives a real browser and works through your product
the way a person would. For a first run, a one-paragraph description of your
audience is plenty.

<Screenshot
  name="actor-create"
  alt="An actor's profile, with its name, description, instructions and location."
  caption="Name the actor by role, not as a person. Instructions say who it is; knowledge is what it knows."
/>

</Step>
<Step>

### Write and run a checklist

To write one yourself, open **Checklists** in the project and choose **Create checklist**. A
checklist is a set of checks written in plain language, for example:

1. Open the pricing page.
2. Confirm the Pro plan shows a monthly and an annual price.
3. Start sign-up and confirm it reaches the email step.

<Screenshot
  name="checklist-editor"
  alt="A checklist being edited, with three plain-language checks listed in order and a Run button."
  caption="One check per outcome. The actor works out the clicks."
/>

Create the checklist, then press **Run** on its page. The actor opens a
hosted browser and works through each check. On the Startup plan and above, a
site only reachable inside your network runs on a Mac in your workspace
instead (see **[Devices](/integrations/self-hosted-workers)**).

</Step>
<Step>

### Review the run

Every run is recorded. Open it to replay the session and inspect each check's
result and screenshot. Share the report with a link, and find failed checks
waiting on the project's **Issues** page.

<Screenshot
  name="checklist-run"
  alt="A finished checklist run: two checks passed and one failed, with the failed check open to show its screenshot and the actor's explanation."
  caption="A failed check opens on the moment it failed, with the actor's reasoning beside the screenshot."
/>

</Step>
</Steps>
