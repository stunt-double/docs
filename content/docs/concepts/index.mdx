# Core concepts

Stunt Double is organized around a small number of building blocks. This page
defines each one and how it relates to the others. For how to use them, follow
the link at the end of each section.

| Term         | In one line                                                                          |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Workspace    | Your team: members, billing and every project                                        |
| Project      | One product or surface, and everything that tests it                                 |
| Actor        | An AI user with instructions and knowledge that drives a real browser                |
| Checklist    | Plain-language checks an actor verifies, each with evidence                          |
| Interview    | A structured study run with persona participants, ending in a report                 |
| Live session | An actor using your product in real time while you watch and steer it (early access) |
| Automation   | A graph of steps that runs checklists on a schedule or an event                      |

| Issue        | A problem a run, review or comment found, tracked until it is fixed                  |

| Idea         | A recommendation your actors write after exploring the product together              |
| Topology     | A map of your product's screens and the paths between them                           |
| Device       | A Mac in your workspace that runs work against sites inside your network             |

## Workspaces and projects

A **workspace** is the top-level container for your team. It owns members,
billing and every project. A **project** sits inside a workspace and groups the
actors, checklists, interviews and runs for one product or surface, so a team
with several products keeps them apart. A project's overview leads with its
latest run, anything running now and what changed since your last visit.

## Actors

An **actor** is an AI user you create, named by the role it stands in for
("Busy shop owner"), never as a person. Three things define it:

- **Instructions**: who it is and how it behaves, written to the actor.
- **Personality**: how skeptical, how technically literate and how quickly
  frustrated it is, set on sliders and applied everywhere it works.
- **Knowledge**: what it can look up mid-run (expertise, processes, reference
  facts and test accounts), opened only when a task needs it.

An actor drives a real browser and works through your product the way a
person would: observe the page, act, observe the result, repeat. On the
Startup plan and above it also gets an inbox of its own for sign-up and
verification emails. See
**[Build an actor that behaves like your audience](/tutorials/first-actor)**.

## Checklists

A **checklist** is a set of checks written in plain language, each describing
an outcome that should be true. An actor runs through them in a browser and
reports each one as passed or failed, with a screenshot and a short
explanation. A check can block the run, add to a score or just record what the
actor saw. Checklists are the fastest way to catch regressions in flows that
are tedious to test by hand, such as sign-up, checkout or onboarding. See
**[Write checklists that catch real regressions](/tutorials/effective-checklists)**.

## Interviews

An **interview** is a structured study run with persona participants: your
actors, or personas generated for the study. You write a guide of questions
and tasks, each participant works through your product and answers in
character, and every round ends in a report of themes, quotes, the screens
behind them and a usability score. See
**[Run a user interview](/tutorials/running-an-interview)**.

## Live sessions

A **live session** puts you beside an actor while it works, so you can steer
it, interview it in character or take over the browser. Live sessions are in
early access and switched on per workspace. See
**[Run a live session](/tutorials/live-sessions)**.

## Workflows

A **workflow**, called an **automation** in the dashboard, is a graph of steps
started by a trigger. Where a checklist verifies one flow, an automation
decides when checklists run and what happens with the result: post a comment
on a pull request, notify a channel, branch on a condition. See
**[Automate multi-step workflows](/tutorials/building-workflows)**.

## Comments and the feedback widget

Actors report what an AI persona experienced. **Comments** cover the other
half: what a real visitor experienced. The **feedback widget** is a small
script you add to your own site, so visitors can drop a pin anywhere on a page
and leave a comment where it applies. Each comment arrives with a screenshot,
the page and device details, and only domains you approve can send them. See
**[Collect stakeholder feedback on a prototype](/tutorials/feedback-widget)**
and the **[feedback widget reference](/integrations/feedback-widget)**.

## Issues, goals and ideas [#insights]

**Issues** are the problems a project needs to fix. Failed checks, open design
review notes and new comments show up on the project's **Issues** page on
their own, each linked to its evidence, and become tracked issues in a click.
See **[Review runs and act on them](/tutorials/reviewing-runs)**.

**Goals** say what a project is trying to achieve, such as "New visitors finish
sign-up without help". Link feedback and ideas to the goal they support, so the
evidence for each goal sits in one place.

**Ideas** come from a co-create session: say what you want to learn and pick
the actors, and each explores the product before they turn what they found into
ideas and recommendations. Problems they find become issues.

## Topology

A **topology** is a map of your product: its screens, the paths between them
and what changed since the last version. An actor crawls the site to draw it,
and pages that checklist runs and interview participants visit are added as
you test. Topology is in preview.

## Devices

A **device** is a Mac in your workspace running the desktop app, on any paid
plan. On the Startup plan and above, runs against a site only reachable inside your network
go through a device, using that Mac's network and sign-ins. See
**[Devices](/integrations/self-hosted-workers)**.

## Recordings and replays

Everything an actor does is recorded. For any run you can replay the session,
step through each action and see the screenshot captured at that moment, which
is what makes a failed check or a surprising interview answer easy to
diagnose.
