# Core concepts

Stunt Double is organized around a small number of building blocks. This page
explains each one and how they fit together.

<ConceptMap />

| Term | In one line |
| --- | --- |
| Workspace | Your team: members, billing and every project |
| Project | One product or surface, and everything that tests it |
| Actor | An AI user with instructions and knowledge that drives a real browser |
| Checklist | Plain-language checks an actor verifies, each with evidence |
| Interview | A structured study run with persona participants, ending in a report |
| Live session | An actor using your product in real time while you watch and steer it (early access) |
| Automation | A graph of steps that runs checklists on a schedule or an event |
| Comment | A pin a real visitor drops on your page through the feedback widget |
| Issue | A problem a run, review or comment found, tracked until it is fixed |
| Idea | A recommendation your actors write after exploring the product together |
| Topology | A map of your product's screens and the paths between them |
| Device | A Mac in your workspace that runs work against sites inside your network |

## Workspaces and projects

A **workspace** is the top-level container for your team. It owns members,
billing, and every project. A **project** sits inside a workspace and groups the
actors, checklists, and runs for one product or surface. Teams working on
several products keep them separated by using a project per product.

A project's overview leads with its latest run, step by step, beside anything
running now, what changed since your last visit and what needs your attention
next.

## Actors

An **actor** is an AI user you create. It is defined by its instructions,
its personality and its knowledge:

- **Instructions** describe the actor's role and how it should behave.
- **Personality** sets how skeptical it is, how technically literate, and how
  quickly it gets frustrated, on sliders. It applies everywhere the actor works.
- **Knowledge** is what the actor can look up: **expertise** it applies,
  **processes** it follows, **reference** facts such as an FAQ or a policy, and
  test **accounts** to sign in with. The actor opens or searches entries as it
  needs them, so it is never handed everything at once.

Under the hood, an actor drives a real browser and works through your product
the way a person would, observing the page, taking an action, observing the
result, and repeating until the task is done. On the Startup plan and above,
each actor gets an inbox of its own, which is what makes sign-up and
verification flows testable, and can stay signed in between runs. Email is the
only channel an actor has: it never uses a phone number or SMS verification,
and reports the step as a blocker instead.

## Checklists

A **checklist** is an automated verification workflow. You describe the steps you
want checked in plain language, and an actor runs through them in a hosted
browser. Each step is reported as passed or failed, with a screenshot and a
short explanation.

Checklists are the fastest way to catch regressions in flows that are tedious to
test by hand, such as signup, checkout, or onboarding.

## Interviews

An **interview** is a structured study run with persona participants: your
actors, or personas generated for the study. You define sections and
questions, each participant works through your product in a real browser and
answers in character, and the round ends in a report of themes, quotes and the
screens behind them. Each participant also answers a standard usability
questionnaire (UMUX), so every round carries a usability score. Share a round's
report with a link.

## Live sessions

A **live session** (in early access, and not yet in every workspace) puts you beside
an actor while it works: the browser on one side, the chat on the other, and the steps it takes underneath. Give it a task
or interview it in character, change direction mid-task, or take the browser
and drive it yourself. Sessions run in a cloud browser or, from the desktop
app, in a browser on your own Mac. See
**[Run a live session](/tutorials/live-sessions)**.

## Workflows

A **workflow** (an **automation** in the dashboard) chains steps into a single repeatable automation. Where a
checklist verifies a flow, a workflow is meant to get something done end to end,
and can combine several actions into one run.

## Comments and the feedback widget

Actors report what an AI persona experienced. **Comments** cover the other half:
what a real visitor experienced. The **feedback widget** is a small script you
add to your own site, letting visitors drop a pin anywhere on a page and leave a
comment in the place it applies to.

Each comment arrives with a screenshot, the page URL, the pin position, and
device details, and lands in the project's Comments view grouped by page. From
there you reply in the thread and move each one through **new**, **reviewed**,
**resolved**, or **dismissed**.

<Callout variant="note" title="Security boundary">
  The widget is identified by a per-project API key that lives in your page source, so the real
  access control is the **domain allowlist** on the project: the widget API only answers requests
  from domains you have approved. Workspace admins can narrow that further, or switch the widget off
  across the whole workspace, from Settings → Security.
</Callout>

See **[Collect stakeholder feedback on a prototype](/tutorials/feedback-widget)**
for installation and triage.

## Issues, goals and ideas

Each project has an **Issues** page. Problems your runs and reviewers have
already found appear there on their own: failed checks, open design review
notes and new comments, each linked back to its evidence. Turn one into a
tracked issue in a click, then move it through open, in progress, resolved or
dismissed. The highest severity sits at the top.

**Goals** say what a project is trying to achieve, such as "New visitors finish
sign-up without help". Link feedback and ideas to the goal they support, so the
evidence for each goal sits in one place. A goal is active until you mark it
completed or archive it.

**Ideas** come from a co-create session: say what you want to learn, pick the
actors, and each explores the product before they turn what they found into
ideas and recommendations. Problems they find become issues.

## Topology

A **topology** is a map of your product: its screens, the paths between them
and what changed since the last version. An actor crawls the site to draw it,
and pages that checklist runs and interview participants go through are added
as you test. Topology is in preview.

## Devices

A **device** is a Mac in your workspace running the desktop app. On the
Startup plan and above, runs against a site only reachable inside your network
go through a device, using that Mac's network and sign-ins, and runs that need
the same device wait their turn. See **[Devices](/integrations/self-hosted-workers)**.

## Recordings and replays

Everything an actor does is recorded. For any run you can replay the session,
step through each action, and see the screenshot captured at that moment. This
is what makes a failed checklist or a surprising interview answer easy to
diagnose.
